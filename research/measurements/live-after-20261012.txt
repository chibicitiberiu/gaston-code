THE THREE THRESHOLDS §8 SET, AND WHAT THEY SAY.

  render_mean > 0.2 x dom_mean          Athlon 14.4%   VM 12.9%   PASS
  total live time per turn up > 10%     Athlon +5.9%   VM +10.8%  one over
  above 5 ms per 4 KB update            Athlon 0.14ms  VM 0.48ms  PASS

TWO OF THOSE THREE ARE THE SAME MEASUREMENT WITH INCONSISTENT LIMITS, and
that is a flaw in how the thresholds were written rather than a finding
about the renderer. "Total up more than 10%" is render_total / dom_before,
and "render_mean > 0.2 x dom_mean" is render_total / dom_after. The two
differ only by the small drift in dom between runs -- so a renderer costing
15% of the DOM write passes the ratio test and fails the total test, every
time, on any machine. The VM landing at 12.9% and +10.8% is exactly that
arithmetic, not a renderer that is nearly too slow.

THE READING. The ratio test is the design question -- is the new work small
against the work that was already there -- and both machines answer yes with
room. The absolute floor is the sanity check and both clear it by a factor
of ten. On the VM the renderer adds 0.43 s of CPU to a 107-second turn,
which is 0.4% of wall clock and is not perceptible. Nothing is throttled.

The ratio DOES bind on the fast machine, as live-before predicted: 14.4% on
the Athlon against 12.9% on the VM, because the VM's DOM is slow for reasons
that are not CPU while the renderer is pure CPU. The TOTAL test binds the
other way, which is the same arithmetic seen from the other end.

WHAT IS WORTH WATCHING. render_max on the VM is 6,083 us -- one update in a
thousand over the 5 ms floor -- against a dom_max of 46,628 us in the same
run. Both are the shape of a 32MB machine that has started swapping rather
than of the code: the run ends with 4.4MB free. On the Athlon, where nothing
swaps, render_max is 507 us and dom_max 1,551.

The Pentium 200 is out of stage 3 by the stage-1 decision, so the margin
between the Athlon and the real target machine is unmeasured and stays that
way until stage 5. These numbers bound the renderer on hardware roughly 5x
the target.
