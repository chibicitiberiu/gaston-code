2. Speed, and the instrument that could not see it
---------------------------------------------------

`probe deflate` CANNOT ANSWER THIS, and the attempt is recorded so it is
not repeated. Three runs of each build, level 1 and level 6:

  otexan   level 1  10, 11, 10 ms      level 6  14, 15, 14 ms
  all-os   level 1  10, 11, 17 ms      level 6  16, 15, 10 ms

GetTickCount resolves about 10 ms and the work takes 10-15 ms, so the
whole difference lies inside one tick. probe.c:479 already says the sample
is one two-line block repeated, compresses 164:1 and "barely has to
search". It is measuring the wrong thing, slowly enough to be invisible.

THE INSTRUMENT THAT WORKS is the one deflate-gremote-vm-20261003.txt used:
a put and a get of the same block through gremote, timed from the host,
five of each, median reported. Payload 377,990 bytes of this project's own
prose and code, 2.59:1 compressible, led by a NUL so the fs lane classifies
it binary and the EOL fold does not rewrite it. Each build was installed
with self_update() and the first build was measured again at the end,
because a single pass cannot tell a regression from a drift:

  otexan      put 0.280s   get 0.176s
  all-os      put 0.261s   get 0.219s
  os-but-hot  put 0.291s   get 0.182s
  otexan (2)  put 0.289s   get 0.178s

The GET is guest -> host: the guest COMPRESSES. That direction costs 23%
at -os (0.177 against 0.219, the baseline reproduced either side of it),
and os-but-hot gives it all back at 0.182.

The PUT is host -> guest: the guest only inflates, and it does not move
outside its own spread (0.280, 0.261, 0.291, 0.289). Inflate is table
lookups; deflate is the search, and the search is what -ot buys.


3. Two modules that were kept at -ot on an argument, and did not earn it
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

Worth more than the bytes, because both arguments sounded right.

  automation/select.o  held at -otexan because "the matcher runs over every
                       node of every scan" (win/Makefile). A 45-node walk:
                       0.045s at -ot, 0.040s at -os, seven calls each. The
                       cost of a tree is cross-process messaging -- about
                       1 ms per node -- and the matcher's string compare is
                       not visible beside it.

  wire/connection.o    held by "the codec". But the codec is miniz; the
                       framing around it is per-frame, not per-byte. The
                       round trip above did not notice it moving.

So the rule that survives is narrower than "hot modules": -ot is for code
that runs over EVERY BYTE. That is miniz's compressor, sync/scanner.cpp's
CRC pass and fs/reader.cpp, and nothing else in this program.


4. The answer
--------------

-os by default; -otexan for miniz.o, sync/scanner.o and fs/reader.o.

win/Makefile is inverted to match: one -ot exception list instead of a
-otexan default with six -os overrides, which is what the evidence had
already turned that default into. gaston -20,992, gremote -13,824, and no
measurable cost in the only direction that showed one.

sync/scanner.o and fs/reader.o are kept on the per-byte rule rather than on
their own timings. Both are reachable only through a sync scan, whose cost
on a 1998 platter is the platter; the flag is worth 394 bytes in scanner.o
and the risk of being wrong is a slower CRC pass over every file synced.
