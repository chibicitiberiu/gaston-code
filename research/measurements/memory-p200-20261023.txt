# What gremote costs this machine under load. The other half of
# docs/REMOTE.md's "gremote memory idle and under load".
#
# Machine : Pentium 200 MMX, Windows 98 SE 4.10.2222 A, 32,284 KB, at
#           10.0.0.158. Freshly booted -- uptime 1,955 s, nothing of ours
#           running but GREMOTE.EXE, no client. That matters: see below.
# Driven  : research/probes/memprobe.py over gremote, sampling SYSINFO from a
#           second thread on the same connection while the load runs on the
#           main one.
# Date    : 2026-10-23

  load     before     min  median   after   n  what ran
  idle       2104    2096    2096    2096  15  3s of nothing
  tree       2096    2096    2096    2080   1  desktop walk, 28 nodes
  shot       2080    1816    1824    1956   5  full-screen 24bpp capture,
                                               1,440,054 bytes
  exec       1956     932     932    1180  42  COMMAND.COM /C DIR C:\ /S,
                                               363,515 bytes of output

Read as differentials between two states of one machine, which is all
dwAvailPhys is good for:

  * **a full-screen 24bpp capture costs about 260 KB** while it is in flight
    and gives most of it back -- 2,080 down to 1,816, back to 1,956. On a
    32 MB box with 2 MB showing free that is an eighth of the apparent
    headroom for one screenshot.
  * **a recursive DIR costs about 1,020 KB** and keeps a third of it: 1,956
    down to 932, back to only 1,180. That is the disk cache growing, not
    gremote, and it is the clearest demonstration in this file of why
    BACKLOG 2 says this field is not headroom.
  * **idle costs nothing measurable**: 2,104 to 2,096 across fifteen samples
    over three seconds is noise.


=== A correction to deflate-p200-20261023.txt, which overclaimed ===

That file says this instrument "measures the disk cache here" and that the
bullet has no usable instrument on this machine, on the strength of
`PROBE.EXE platform` reporting **0 KB free** earlier the same evening. That
was a real reading and the caution behind it is sound -- dwAvailPhys excludes
the cache Win98 shrinks under pressure, so it is not headroom.

But "the instrument does not work here" was too strong, and this run is the
refutation. The zero was a *moment*, on a box that had been up 101 minutes
with the client running, three self_updates, a 155-second walk and a crash
dialog behind it. On a freshly booted machine the field is 2,112 and moves
coherently with the load: a screenshot and a recursive directory walk
produce different, repeatable, explicable deltas.

So the honest statement is narrower than either: **the field is usable when
it is not already floored, and it tells you nothing when it is.** A figure
from it needs the before-reading quoted beside it, which is why this tool
always takes one.

The `n` column earns its place here too. `tree` got a single sample -- the
walk was 28 nodes and finished inside one 0.2 s interval -- so its "min" is
just the before-reading. memprobe prints the warning for exactly that case,
and the warning's text ("should not happen on a P200") is now wrong: it does
happen, when the desktop is quiet and the client is not running.


=== What is still not measured ===

The **no-gremote baseline** cannot be taken over the wire by construction:
asking gremote how much memory is free without gremote requires gremote not
to be running. That is `PROBE.EXE platform` at the keyboard, and
`--baseline KB` folds it in when somebody has it.

And every figure above is **with no client**. REMOTE.md asks for the
with-and-without-client pair; this is the without half.


=== The with-client half, and it explains the zero ===

Same box, same session, client launched and left to settle:

  free with gremote only          1,616 KB
  free with the client as well        8 KB

and the loads re-run with both up:

  load     before     min  median   after   n  what ran
  idle         24       0      20       0  14  3s of nothing
  shot          0       0      44     336   6  full-screen 24bpp capture
  exec        336       0      12     244  42  COMMAND.COM /C DIR C:\ /S

**The client costs about 1.6 MB of dwAvailPhys and takes the field to
essentially nothing.** That is the answer to REMOTE.md's with-and-without
pair, and it is also the explanation for the 0 KB that
deflate-p200-20261023.txt recorded from `PROBE.EXE platform` earlier in the
evening: the client was running then. It was never a transient and never a
mystery -- it was this.


=== So correct the correction, which was also too broad ===

The section above says "the field is usable when it is not already floored",
and that is right, but it implies the flooring is incidental. It is not. On
this machine the floored state is the NORMAL state once the client is up,
which is the state the product actually runs in. So:

  * **without the client**, memprobe produces coherent, explicable deltas --
    a screenshot costs ~260 KB, a recursive DIR ~1,020 KB.
  * **with the client**, the field sits at 0-336 KB and the deltas are noise.
    `shot` "costs" nothing and ends 336 KB better off than it started; `idle`
    reads 24, 20, 0. These are not measurements of anything.

Which means the original caution in deflate-p200-20261023.txt was closer to
right than the correction that followed it, for the case that matters. The
instrument answers "what does the client cost" very clearly -- 1.6 MB -- and
cannot answer "what does a screenshot cost while the client is up", because
there is no room left for the answer to move in.

Three statements in three hours, each narrower than the last. The final one:
**dwAvailPhys measures this product's headroom exactly once, when the client
starts, and is useless afterwards.**


=== And none of it is a problem, which is the point of BACKLOG 2 ===

With 8 KB of dwAvailPhys showing, this machine ran a complete tool-calling
turn in five seconds (wire-p200-20261023.txt), took three full-screen
captures, and walked its desktop. Nothing failed to allocate. BACKLOG 2
records the project being frightened by this field once before -- the "28 KB
free after a turn" scare -- and concluding that allocations were never
actually failing. This is the same number arriving again, on real hardware,
with the same meaning: Win98 gives the disk cache back under pressure and
dwAvailPhys does not count it.

The figure that WOULD say something about headroom is the one `PROBE.EXE
platform` prints beside it and gremote does not expose: `free resources:
system 80%, GDI 91%, USER 80%`. Those are the heaps that kill 9x
applications, and they were comfortable all evening. Getting them over the
wire is a new control-lane field, which is a change for whoever next touches
that lane.
