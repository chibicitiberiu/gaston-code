And two where Wine is simply wrong about the platform:

  watch-can-skip-our-own-process   0 kept, 0 dropped by our own filter.
      Win98 honours WINEVENT_SKIPOWNPROCESS at the system level; Wine
      accepts the flag and delivers the events anyway, which is why the
      filter is also implemented in the callback.

  wait-for-gone-sees-a-destruction  1029ms, 2 scans, events 2 -> 3.
      Win98 DOES emit EVENT_OBJECT_DESTROY. Wine never does, and the wait
      backstop is what covered that; here the backstop reports "not
      exercised" because the feed answered.

=== the hung-thread tiers, on the guest ===

  one thread, four wedged windows, 200ms budget : 275 ms   (naive 800)
  two wedged threads, 600ms budget              : 777 ms   (naive 1200)

777 is one full budget plus one quarter, which is the middle tier doing
what it claims. Under Wine the same two cases were 205 and 754.

One assertion had to change, and only the guest could have shown it:
`deferredNodes()` counts windows ASKED at a reduced budget, and one of
those can still ANSWER. Under Wine every deferred window also timed out, so
`deferred == timeouts - 1` held by accident. The guest walked three,
deferred two, and only two timed out. The invariant is about who pays, not
who refuses: `deferred == walked - 1`.

=== 0.15f  targeted injection -- THE PLAN WAS WRONG ===

  WH_CALLWNDPROC     installed; +0 calls, +0 distinct pids
  WH_GETMESSAGE      installed; +23 calls, +1 distinct pids
  WH_CBT             installed; +0 calls, +0 distinct pids
  WH_CALLWNDPROCRET  installed; +0 calls, +0 distinct pids

  -> TARGETED INJECTION WORKS: our code ran in the target and in no
     other process.

The plan specified WH_CALLWNDPROC and asserted it maps into one process.
It installs and never fires. So do WH_CBT and WH_CALLWNDPROCRET aimed at a
thread. Only WH_GETMESSAGE delivers -- and it fires inside GetMessage, so
the controller must POST as well as send.

Built on the documented choice, the whole in-process tier would have been
silently dead, and it would have looked like a bug in the collector.

=== 0.15g  which text call each module actually makes ===

  (this exe)     TextOutA
  MSHTML.DLL     TextOutW ExtTextOutW ExtTextOutA DrawTextW DrawTextA
  COMCTL32.DLL   ExtTextOutW ExtTextOutA DrawTextExA
  USER32.DLL     no text entry point imported by name
  COMDLG32.DLL   DrawTextA ExtTextOutA TextOutA
  SHELL32.DLL    ExtTextOutA
  RICHED32.DLL   TextOutA ExtTextOutA

MSHTML is the module D4 exists for and it imports DrawTextW and DrawTextA.
DrawText* is USER32, not GDI32, and was not patched at all. A web page and
a dialog would each have been captured in part and reported as the text on
screen -- worse than capturing nothing, because it looks like an answer.

Under Wine this same gate said COMCTL32 imports only the W forms of
TextOut/ExtTextOut and that MSHTML imports nothing by name. Both halves of
that are wrong about Windows. Eight entry points are patched now.
